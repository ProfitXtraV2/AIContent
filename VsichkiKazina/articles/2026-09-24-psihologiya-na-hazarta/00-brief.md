## Angle
Cognitive traps that make gambling feel winnable when the odds are permanently against the player. Explain four behavioural-science phenomena in plain Bulgarian and tie them to why knowing them is protective:
1. Заблуда на комарджията (gambler's fallacy) — belief that past independent outcomes shift future odds. RNG events are independent; „почервеня 5 пъти, сега е ред на черното" is false.
2. „Дължи ми се" / the „I'm due" fallacy — the same error aimed at oneself; nothing is owed.
3. Ефект на почти-печалбата (near-miss) — two of three jackpot symbols recruit the same reward circuitry as a real win and push continued play, but a near-miss is just a loss.
4. Илюзия за контрол — buttons, stop-reels, ritual choices feel like skill but do not change RNG odds.
5. Гоненето на загуби (loss chasing) — the trap that pulls all three together; the „I'm due" logic scaled up.

## RESEARCH — sources (real web research, 24.09.2026)

**Gambler's fallacy (заблуда на комарджията / Monte Carlo fallacy)**
- The false belief that past results change the odds of the next independent event. A roulette wheel „has no memory"; the next spin's odds are unchanged regardless of the run before it. (addictionhelp.com; Wikipedia, Gambler's fallacy)
- Origin of the name: Monte Carlo Casino, 18.08.1913 — the wheel landed on black 26 times in a row (probability roughly 1 in ~66.6 million); as the run lengthened, players piled money on red believing it „overdue" and lost fortunes. This is the documented historical origin of the „Monte Carlo fallacy" name. (Wikipedia, Gambler's fallacy; casinoedges.net)

**Near-miss effect (ефект на почти-печалбата)**
- Neuroscience: near-misses (e.g. two of three jackpot symbols, the third just off) recruit the same win-related reward circuitry (ventral striatum) as actual wins, even though a near-miss is objectively a loss. Clark, Lawrence, Astley-Jones & Gray (2009), „Gambling Near-Misses Enhance Motivation to Gamble and Recruit Win-Related Brain Circuitry", Neuron. (sciencedirect.com S0896627309000373)
- Near-misses were rated as more unpleasant than full misses yet increased the desire to keep playing; designers include them deliberately to create the sense of being „close". (frontiersin.org; teachboston.org; cachecreek.com near-miss-effect)
- Dopamine modulates reward expectancy after a near-miss (D2-receptor activity), which can amplify the effect. (Nature/Neuropsychopharmacology, npp2010230)
- [VERIFY: „~30% near-miss frequency maximises persistence" — reported in some studies (Kassinove & Schare); kept conceptual and omitted as a hard figure to stay within brief's „concepts not numbers".]

**Illusion of control (илюзия за контрол)**
- Superficial game features — a choice, an instrumental response (pressing a button, stopping a reel, picking numbers), a ritual — promote an erroneous perception of skill over outcomes determined only by chance. Rooted in Ellen Langer's „illusion of control" work (1975). (medium/@alexpeterson; UBC Clark & Wohl 2021 PDF on Langer's Illusion of Control; rg.org Immunize Nevada)
- These features do not change the underlying odds; the outcome is set by the RNG regardless of how the button is pressed.

**Loss chasing (гонене на загуби)**
- Continuing or intensifying gambling to recoup previous losses. Traditionally the defining feature marking the transition from non-problem to problem gambling; among the most reliably present clinical markers in people with a gambling-disorder diagnosis. (ScienceDirect S2352154619301068; PMC10628006; gamblingharm.org)
- [VERIFY: „endorsed by >82% of those with a gambling-disorder diagnosis" — cited by PMC10628006; kept qualitative in the piece rather than as a hard figure.]

**Frame:** all four are cognitive distortions that make a fixed-odds, house-edge game feel winnable. Knowing them is protective — that is the article's thesis and its RG spine.
