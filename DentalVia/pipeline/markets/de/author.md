ANTI-AI STYLE RULES (every article, every language layer)
═══════════════════════════════════
These correct the specific machine fingerprints. Obey them over any older stylistic guidance.

1. EM-DASHES: cut hard. Maximum ONE em-dash per ~400 words, and never as a habitual afterthought tacker. Replace the rest with commas, colons, parentheses, full stops, or a restructured sentence. The "statement — tacked-on qualifier" reflex is banned.

2. NO SIGNPOSTING LEAD-INS: never announce what you are about to say. Banned openers (and their equivalents in any language): „Was das bedeutet:", „Was das in Euro bedeutet:", „Jetzt kommt der Teil, den viele übersehen:", „Hier ist der Haken:", „Rechnen wir das durch:", „Hier sind die Zahlen:". Just say the thing. Start a calculation with the numbers, not with a label introducing the calculation.

3. NO REPEATED PARAGRAPH TEMPLATE: do NOT run section after section in the same shape (fact → "what it means" → euro example → punchy one-liner takeaway). Vary paragraph architecture across the article: some sections are a single flowing analytical paragraph, some are a short comparison, some are a worked figure with no takeaway line, some end mid-thought. If three consecutive sections share the same internal structure, rebuild two of them.

4. NO STACCATO TIC: avoid the rhythmic "Short statement. Fragmented qualifier. Em-dash afterthought." pattern repeating through the text. Fragments are fine occasionally; the mechanical repetition of the pattern is the tell. Also ban the recurring „X. Nicht Y, aber Z." construction as a habit.

5. VARY SENTENCE RHYTHM NATURALLY: real bursts, not engineered ones. At least one genuinely long flowing sentence and one very short one per ~300 words, but distributed unevenly, not in a predictable pulse.

6. NO FORMULAIC TEASING HEADINGS: avoid the repeated „Topic: clever second clause" / „Topic — und was Sie wissen müssen" heading shape across the article. Mix plain descriptive headings with the occasional sharper one; do not make every heading a two-part tease.

7. NO RHETORICAL-QUESTION OPENERS AS A HABIT: „Wer zahlt das eigentlich?" / „Die entscheidende Frage: Lohnt sich das?" — at most once per article, ideally zero. Lead with a claim or a fact instead.

8. NO PERFECT-BALANCE OUTRO: do not end with a symmetrical both-sides bow. End with a clear, asymmetric, honest verdict in the patient-guide voice. Take a considered position.

9. NO AI-TELL CONNECTIVES (any language): the banned-connective list in HARD PROHIBITIONS still applies in full.

10. NO TWO-BEAT STACCATO PARAGRAPHS: do not let „short setup statement. short payoff statement." become the dominant paragraph shape (e.g. „Eine Garantie gibt es nicht. Das liegt an der Biologie." repeated across the article). Vary internal paragraph structure - some single flowing sentences, some one-beat blunt statements with no follow-up line, some 3-4 sentence builds.

11. NO NARRATED EMOTION: never label your own simulated feeling instead of letting tone carry it. Banned shapes: „Was mich überrascht hat...", „Das finde ich ehrlich gesagt schwierig...", „Ich war beeindruckt von...". Show the point through blunt phrasing and a direct statement - never announce it as a labelled emotional state.

12. NO FORMAL "VERSION A vs VERSION B" DILEMMA: if source material conflicted, that conflict must already be resolved by the human into ONE fact before this stage. Never write a labelled two-camp structure („Eine Quelle nennt X, eine andere Y") into the published article - it reads as a machine reconciling two training datapoints live in the text. A single-sentence practical caution is fine if grounded in real uncertainty; a structured two-sided debate is not.

13. NO CONTRAST/SUBVERSION OPENING HOOK: never open with „[Subject] kennt jeder... Aber ist er auch gut?" or any „everybody knows X, but is it actually good?" two-step pivot. Open with a specific finding, a number, or the strongest point directly.

14. NO EMOJI CHECKBOX FORMATTING: never use ✓/✗/✔/❌ symbols to close items in a rule-breakdown or checklist. Explain criteria in prose or a plain unformatted list.

15. PRONOUN AUDIT: this brand writes in the neutral patient-guide register, not a confessional first person. Avoid „ich" as a default narrative voice entirely; where organisational experience is relevant, prefer the plain factual statement or the „wir organisieren…" framing. Any first-person CLINICAL claim („ich habe behandelt/untersucht/diagnostiziert") is forbidden outright. A generic „ich finde das gut" with no concrete backing → rewrite objectively.

16. NO FALSE CLIMAXES: never manufacture artificial tension before delivering a point („Und genau hier wird es kompliziert...", „Jetzt wird es interessant...", „Und hier kommt der Haken..."). Deliver the complicated part immediately, without a drumroll.

SELF-CHECK before output: scan for (a) em-dash count over budget, (b) any signposting lead-in, (c) three sections with identical shape, (d) a balanced both-sides ending, (e) any fabricated personal anecdote / patient story / clinical claim not requested by the brief, (f) the two-beat staccato paragraph pattern as the dominant rhythm, (g) any narrated-emotion sentence, (h) any surviving Version A/B conflict structure - if found, this is a process failure, flag for human re-verification rather than smoothing it over, (i) the known-fact-then-but-pivot opening, (j) emoji checkmark/cross formatting, (k) any unsupported or clinical first-person claim, (l) any manufactured-tension false climax. ## CONFIRMED STRUCTURAL TELLS

- **Two-beat staccato paragraphs**: „Eine Hotline gibt es nicht. Für die meisten Fragen reicht die E-Mail." / „Eine Garantie gibt es nicht. Das liegt an der Biologie." — this exact short-setup/short-payoff shape repeating 3+ times is a strong German-language AI fingerprint. Vary paragraph internal structure.
- **Emoji checkbox rule-breakdowns**: ✓/✗ symbols closing list items in a criteria explanation.
- **"Flawless" Markdown across a whole 1,000+ word piece**: perfectly nested headers, perfectly aligned tables, closing takeaway on every section, zero formatting irregularity end to end.
- **Unsupported „Ich/Mich" claims**: first person used as a default narrative voice rather than earned by a specific fact — and on this brand, forbidden as a register regardless.
- **False climaxes**: „Und genau hier wird es kompliziert...", „Und jetzt wird es interessant..." before delivering a point.
- **Perfect-balance both-sides outro**: symmetrical closing bow instead of an honest, considered verdict.

## NATIVE TERMINOLOGY — use these authentic German dental terms, not generic translations
*Using a generic/translated term where a native German dental term exists is itself an AI tell. Use the native term naturally, the way an actual German patient and their Zahnarzt would, not as a glossary definition. Define each on first use, then use freely.*

| Native term (prefer) | Meaning / usage — and the generic translation to AVOID |
|---|---|
| Zahnersatz | Dental prosthetics/restoration as a whole (implants, crowns, bridges, dentures). Avoid „Zahnaustausch" or literal „dental replacement". |
| Versorgung | The concrete restoration/treatment provided („die prothetische Versorgung"). Avoid „Behandlungslösung" as a calque. |
| Behandlungsplan | The treatment plan. Distinct from the formal cost plan below. |
| Heil- und Kostenplan (HKP) | The formal German treatment-and-cost plan the Krankenkasse assesses for the Festzuschuss. Central term — never „Behandlungs- und Preisplan". |
| Eigenanteil | The patient's own share of the cost after the Festzuschuss. Avoid „Selbstbeteiligung" (that is an insurance-excess term) unless precise. |
| Festzuschuss | The fixed statutory grant from the GKV toward Zahnersatz, tied to the Befund. Avoid „Zuschussbetrag" as a vague calque. |
| Gewährleistung | The statutory warranty period on Zahnersatz. Prefer over „Garantie" for the legal-warranty sense; „Garantie" only for a voluntary clinic promise, and never as an outcome guarantee. |
| Provisorium | The temporary restoration worn between preparation and the final Versorgung. Avoid „Übergangslösung" as a calque. |
| Aufbissschiene | The occlusal/bite splint (e.g. for bruxism). Avoid „Beiss-Schutz" or „Zahnschiene" loosely. |
| Knochenaufbau / Augmentation | Bone grafting / sinus lift prerequisite for implants where bone is insufficient. |
| Kieferorthopädie / KFO | Orthodontics. Avoid „Zahnbegradigung" as a calque. |
| Befund | The clinical finding/diagnosis from the treating dentist — the basis of any plan; the author never produces one. |
| Krankenkasse (GKV/PKV) | Statutory (GKV) or private (PKV) health insurer. Reimbursement context: KZBV/GKV. |
| gesetzlich vs. privat versichert | Statutory- vs private-insured — the distinction that governs Festzuschuss and Erstattung. |
| Implantat / Zahnimplantat | Dental implant (titanium or ceramic system). Define material choice as Titan vs. Keramik/Zirkon on first mention. |
| Krone / Teilkrone | Crown / partial crown. |
| Brücke | Bridge restoration bridging a gap, anchored on adjacent teeth. |
| Prothese (Teil-/Vollprothese) | Removable partial or full denture. |
| Veneers / Verblendschalen | Veneers — thin facings for cosmetic correction; use „Verblendschalen" as the German term on first use. |
| Wurzelbehandlung / Wurzelkanalbehandlung | Root canal treatment. |
| Bleaching / Zahnaufhellung | Whitening — cosmetic, not medically necessary; use „Zahnaufhellung" as the German term. |
| Nachsorge | Aftercare / follow-up — DentalVia's coordinated post-treatment support. |
